# Cartoon Animal Explainer preview provenance

Seven previews are declared by two of this collection's three standalone Content Packs. **All seven now exist**, rendered on 2026-09-09 under the owner's authorisation of that date. This file records why, exactly what was run to produce them, and the prompt text of each, so a later reader can reproduce the run rather than guess at it.

The pack that declares no raster assets — `com.storystudio.metadata-formula.every-type-explained-titles` — is complete: it builds deterministically, signs, and passes `validateContentPack` under an ephemeral authoring key. The other two were **blocked on previews only** and are no longer blocked; their documents already validate against the signed Content Pack schema, which was checked directly by building each of them once in a throwaway directory under `/tmp` with disposable rasters in place. Those rasters were deleted and no placeholder byte was written into this repository.

## Three previews per visual style

The owner's rule, set on 2026-09-08 and applied to Royal History first, carries forward here: every Visual Style declares **three** previews rather than one, and the three are chosen so a buyer sees the style where it is easy, where it is quiet, and where it is hard.

- **hero** — the shot the style exists for, the one a cover would use.
- **scene** — a quiet wide scene with **no animal in it at all**, so a buyer can see what the look does to an empty landscape. For a style whose whole subject is one animal, this is the honest hard question: the backdrop is deliberately reduced to a horizon, one tree and a rock, and the scene preview shows what is left when the animal is taken out of it.
- **face** — the hardest case the style can be asked for, chosen inside the style's own contract rather than against it.
  - Flat Cartoon Wildlife exists to carry an animal on a thick clean outline, a flat muted fill and a soft glow, with no gradient anywhere else and no texture at all. Its hardest case is therefore a long-haired animal's head held close: at that range there is no silhouette to rescue the drawing, no landscape to separate it, and a coat that every other style would render as fur has to be built out of a handful of flat shapes and one outline. If the look survives one shaggy head, it survives everything else the recipe will ask of it.

The Thumbnail Recipe declares four, as the Lost Voyage, Royal History, Golden Epic, Midnight Archives, Sleep Theories and Gory Cartoon History recipes did, so that the fixed frame is shown against four different subjects and a buyer can see what actually varies between episodes. Two of the four show the base variant with its three one-word labels, and two show the Flags variant, so both are shown twice rather than one being inferred from the other.

## Under what authorisation these were rendered

`docs/VISUAL_STYLE_CONTENT_PACK_FORMAT.md` and the `story-studio-visual-style-adoption` skill both state that this control Mac verifies preview handoffs and does not perform production image generation, and that previews come only from owner-supplied original bytes or a production-preview handoff. That standing rule is lifted only by an explicit owner authorisation given in chat for one named pass, as it was for the earlier collections on 2026-09-08. **This collection's previews were rendered under such an authorisation, given on 2026-09-09**, which named the model (`gpt-image-2` through GenStudio, no other route), named this run id, set a credit ceiling for the whole pass, and instructed that the results be published without a further confirmation. Nothing was substituted and no placeholder byte was ever written to let a pack pass validation dishonestly: the runs below are the only source of these seven files.

## The exact command a keyed operator must run

Same route the earlier collections used: the Image Pool generator inside a running Story Studio app, in a project whose configured image product is the GenStudio text-to-image lane for `gpt-image-2`. Seven prompts, one variant each, aspect `16:9`, `reference_images: []` (text-only, no reference asset, no image-to-image, no compositing, no post-render typography — the three labels in the recipe previews are drawn by the image model, as the recipe contract requires).

```
POST /api/projects/pack-previews/image-pool/generate
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "pool_id": "pack-previews",
  "model": "gpt-image-2",
  "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
  "resolution": "2K",
  "variants": 1,
  "run_id": "cartoon-animal-explainer-previews-1",
  "prompts": [ ...the seven prompts below, in order... ]
}
```

That is the request as it was actually sent, on 2026-09-09, against Story Studio at `http://127.0.0.1:8766`. It returned job `a267c22d`, on which only prompts 1 and 2 succeeded: the GenStudio image lane went into a `direct provider is temporarily unavailable` outage partway through the batch and refused the remaining five submissions, releasing 29 credits for each. The outage is recorded in full under "The provider outage of 2026-09-09" below, together with the three retry runs that carried the remaining five prompts to completion. In every job the executed `params.prompts` were compared against the prompt bodies below by SHA-256, in order, and are identical; that comparison was made while each job record was still live, because Story Studio prunes a finished job's history once GenStudio confirms every provider task has settled.

Then poll the returned job, retain the returned PNGs outside the repository, and convert each accepted original with a whole-image resize (cover, lanczos3) plus WebP quality 90 effort 6 to the dimensions in the table — no crop beyond the sub-pixel aspect trim, no overlay, composite, or repair, falling back to lossless effort 6 for any file landing under the 20,480-byte floor. Record the original digest and byte count, the accepted digest, byte count and decode result, and the credits spent, in the table below before committing the bytes.

### Request size

These seven prompts serialise to **13,439 bytes**, which is inside the 16 KiB body cap that blocked the thirteen-prompt Midnight Archives batch on Story Studio 1.4.128 and earlier. Send them as one job with the run id above; the cap is why this collection's body is held to seven prompts per run id. Do not shorten, merge or reword a prompt to save bytes — the prompt text is the contract. ## The provider outage of 2026-09-09, and the three retry runs

Recorded rather than smoothed over, because the next operator will meet it. The seven prompts went out as one job (`run_id` `cartoon-animal-explainer-previews-1`, job `a267c22d`). Prompts 1 and 2 returned and were accepted. **Prompts 3 through 7 were refused at submission** — `IMAGE_PROVIDER_SUBMISSION_FAILED`, provider reason `direct provider is temporarily unavailable`, with Story Studio reporting `GenStudio reports 29 credits released` for each. This is not a safety refusal and not a fault in the prompt text: nothing about these five prompts caused it, the same wording succeeded unchanged later, and no credit was consumed by any of the five.

A refused submission releases its credits, so the remedy is simply to resubmit the missing prompts under a fresh run id. The pool names files `gen-<run_id>-NNNN.png` with the index following the prompt's position **in that job**, so a distinct run id per attempt is what keeps a retry from overwriting an earlier file and what makes the mapping in "Retained bytes" provable. ## The rejected first return, kept as history

`labelled-caricature-trio-4.webp` was rendered twice, and the first return was **thrown away rather than committed**. It arrived on job `16d2210b` (`gen-cartoon-animal-explainer-previews-1-retry-0005.png`) and drew, above the middle parrot, an unmistakable **flag of France** — a navy, white and red vertical tricolour. This collection's acceptance rules refuse exactly that: "a returned image that reproduces … a real national flag … must be rejected rather than committed." The three birds and the rest of the frame were otherwise correct, and it would have been easy to keep. It was rejected because the rule is the product: a Flags variant whose whole promise is invented flags cannot ship a real one.

The prompt was **not reworded**. It already says "no real national flag" and "each drawn flat with a thick clean outline as two or three plain blocks of colour and no emblem, no crest, no star, no symbol and no lettering on the cloth"; the model drifted against wording that was already correct, so changing the contract would have been the wrong repair. It was resubmitted unchanged under `run_id` `cartoon-animal-explainer-previews-1-retry4` (job `656c780f`), and the executed prompt was confirmed byte-identical to prompt 7 below by SHA-256 before the return was accepted. The second return carries three invented flags — a tan-and-green horizontal bicolour, a grey, white and red vertical tricolour, and a teal, cream and blue vertical tricolour — and none of them is a real national flag.

The rejected image is not in this repository and its 29 credits are a real cost of this pass, counted below. The lesson for a re-run: **the flags variant is where this recipe drifts.** A vertical tricolour is the most canonical flag shape a model knows, and blue-white-red is the most canonical colouring of it, so every future render of prompt 5 or prompt 7 must be checked against real national flags before it is committed.

## Acceptance rules specific to this collection

Original subjects only. Every animal in these prompts is described from scratch as an ordinary member of a real species, and must be drawn from the description; a returned image that reproduces a recognisable character, a real person, a company mark, a sports crest, a real national flag, an invented insignia presented as genuine, or a copied composition must be rejected rather than committed. No human being, no hand and no bare skin appears in any prompt in this collection, and a returned image containing any of them must be rejected rather than committed. Every animal must be calm: an image showing an animal frightened, injured, bleeding, hunting, being hunted, or in any visible distress must be rejected rather than committed, and so must any image whose anatomy is wrong for the species it names. In the four recipe previews the one-word labels must be spelled exactly as written, unclipped at both ends, each on one line and each sitting directly above its own animal; no other word may appear anywhere in the frame, and the two Flags previews must carry no word at all.

## Prompt 1 — `flat-cartoon-wildlife-hero.webp` (style preview, hero, no text)

> A flat cartoon wildlife illustration, sixteen by nine, drawn rather than rendered. One animal only: a single adult plains zebra stands in full profile a little right of centre, whole, all four feet on the ground, head level and turned slightly towards the viewer, ears forward, eyes simple and open, mouth a short relaxed line. It is calm and standing still. Every shape in the picture is closed by a thick clean black outline of even weight, one smooth confident contour per form, slightly heavier on the outer silhouette than on the interior detail, and every contour closes. All colour is flat and muted with one hue per surface — chalky cream, warm charcoal, dusty sage, faded ochre, soft slate — and where the animal needs a second value it is a second flat shape of a slightly darker muted tone with a hard edge, never a blend and never a rendered highlight. The stripes are drawn as flat closed shapes, not as texture. The backdrop is a simple landscape reduced to as few shapes as it can survive on: one flat band of dusty sage ground meeting one flat band of pale sky on a straight low horizon, a single flat-topped acacia tree well to the left, one rounded grey rock near the right edge, and three small tufts of grass. Behind the animal sits one soft radial glow in a lighter tint of the sky colour, centred on it and fading out well before the frame edge; it is the only gradient anywhere in the picture and it carries no second hue and no ring. Everything else is completely flat: no texture, no grain, no noise, no brush marks, no halftone, no airbrush, no cast shadow, no rim light. Camera level and at the animal's own height, with generous flat empty space above and to the sides. No text, no lettering, no numerals, no watermark, no signature, no border, no logo, no invented insignia, no second animal, no human figure, no hand, no bare skin, no recognisable character, no injury, no bared teeth.
