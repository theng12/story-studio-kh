## A collection with no channel

Neither of these two Visual Styles belongs to a channel. Both declare `compatible_formula_ids: []`, exactly as the shipped Production Looks and Storytelling Looks styles do, because a look that is sold on its own must not claim a Series Formula it was not authored against. That is the whole reason this collection exists as its own directory rather than being folded into either of the two channel collections in this batch.

## Three previews per visual style

The owner's rule, set on 2026-09-08 and applied to Royal History first, carries forward here: every Visual Style declares **three** previews rather than one, and the three are chosen so a buyer sees the style where it is easy, where it is quiet, and where it is hard.

- **hero** — the shot the style exists for, the one a cover would use.
- **scene** — a quiet wide scene with **no figure in it at all**, so a buyer can see what the look does to an empty room.
- **face** — the hardest case the style can be asked for, chosen inside the style's own contract rather than against it.
  - Inked Fable Cartoon carries its menace in small glowing white eyes set deep under a heavy brow, with the muzzle and the hands doing the acting rather than large cartoon eyes. Its hardest case is therefore one animal head held close, where the expression has to be built from brow, muzzle, ear and two soft-edged cel steps alone, with a single amber key and nothing to lift the shadow.
  - Scratchy Editorial Poster is built for a tightly cropped subject on stark white and works in two inks with no gradient of any kind. Its hardest case is the thing scratchy hatching fails at first: a face at close crop, where every value of the skin has to come from spacing and crossing of pen strokes over a rough brush block, with the paper left bare for every lit plane and no smooth tone anywhere on the frame.

Neither pack is a Thumbnail Recipe or a lettering style, so the four-preview recipe figure and the one-swatch lettering figure have nothing to bind here.

## Why nothing was rendered in the authoring pass

`docs/VISUAL_STYLE_CONTENT_PACK_FORMAT.md` and the `story-studio-visual-style-adoption` skill both state that this control Mac verifies preview handoffs and does not perform production image generation, and that previews come only from owner-supplied original bytes or a production-preview handoff. The 2026-09-06 sleep-history pass rendered its six previews under an explicit owner authorisation given in chat for that pass only. No such authorisation existed when this collection was authored, so nothing was generated, nothing was substituted, and no placeholder bytes were written that would let a pack pass validation dishonestly. **The authorisation arrived on 2026-09-08** and all six were rendered in that pass; what ran is recorded below.

## The exact command a keyed operator must run (and did)

Same route the sleep-history previews used: the Image Pool generator inside a running Story Studio app, in a project whose configured image product is the GenStudio text-to-image lane for `gpt-image-2`. Six prompts, one variant each, aspect `16:9`, `reference_images: []` (text-only, no reference asset, no image-to-image, no compositing, no post-render typography).

```
POST /api/projects/<project-slug>/image-pool/generate
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "pool_id": "<image pool id>",
  "model": "<published GenStudio gpt-image-2 product id>",
  "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
  "resolution": "<published output row, 1K or higher>",
  "variants": 1,
  "run_id": "standalone-looks-previews-1",
  "prompts": [ ...the six prompts below, in order... ]
}
```

Then poll the returned job, retain the returned PNGs outside the repository, and convert each accepted original with a whole-image resize (cover, lanczos3) to the dimensions in the table — no crop beyond the sub-pixel aspect trim, no overlay, composite, or repair. What actually ran used WebP quality 90 effort 6, the house encoder the sleep-history, Lost Voyage, Royal History and Midnight Archives passes all used, falling back to lossless effort 6 for any file landing under the 20,480-byte floor at quality 90; none did — the smallest encoded to 174,636 bytes — so quality 90 stands for all six. Record the original digest and byte count, the accepted digest, byte count and decode result, and the credits spent, in the table below before committing the bytes.

### Request size

These six prompts serialise to **11,046 bytes**, which is inside the 16 KiB body cap that blocked the thirteen-prompt Midnight Archives batch on Story Studio 1.4.128 and earlier. Send them as one job with the run id above. Do not shorten, merge or reword a prompt to save bytes — the prompt text is the contract. The credits are 6 × 29 = 174.

**That is what was done.** The whole request body measured 11,068 bytes and was accepted as one job. `c55d3e01` ran against the `pack-previews` project on 2026-09-08 and returned `gen-standalone-looks-previews-1-0001.png` through `-0004.png` and `-0006.png` for prompts 1-4 and 6. It recorded `gpt-image-2`, aspect `16:9`, resolution `2K`, one variant each, and every returned original decodes as 2560x1440 PNG.

### One item failed in transport

Recorded here rather than smoothed over, because the next operator will meet it. Prompt 5 (`scratchy-editorial-poster-scene.webp`) did not render. GenStudio returned

```
IMAGE_PROVIDER_SUBMISSION_UNCERTAIN
```

for that one item after roughly six minutes, Story Studio reported `GenStudio reports 29 credits released`, and the job ended `error` with `5/6 required item(s) verified`. Nothing about the prompt caused it — the same prompt text succeeded unchanged on the next attempt — and no other item in the job was affected.

The five that had landed were kept. Prompt 5 was resubmitted **on its own, unaltered**, under the distinct run id `standalone-looks-previews-1b`, because the pool names files `gen-<run_id>-NNNN.png` with the index restarting at 1 for each job: resending the whole six-prompt body under the original run id would have overwritten the five good files and spent 174 credits to replace work that was already correct. Job `da15eecf` reported `done` and returned `gen-standalone-looks-previews-1b-0001.png`, which decodes as 2560x1440 PNG and carries the same job parameters.

The money is therefore 5 × 29 charged on `c55d3e01`, 29 released for the failed item, and 29 charged on `da15eecf` — **174 credits net for six files, exactly as costed**, with 203 gross submitted and 29 given back.

The six prompts recorded below are byte-identical to the executed jobs' `params.prompts` — prompts 1-6 against `c55d3e01` and prompt 5 again against `da15eecf`, checked string by string against `jobs.json`, all identical — which is what makes the file-to-prompt mapping in "Retained bytes" a fact rather than an assumption.

### Original subjects only

Every subject in these six prompts is invented for this pass: no named character, no existing illustration, no living or historical person, no real publication and no real place. The Scratchy Editorial Poster prompts additionally require that the large background capitals be **broken letter fragments that spell no word in any language**, because the style's contract treats that lettering as texture and leaves real titles to the caption system. A returned image whose background lettering resolves into a readable word, or whose subject resembles an existing character, must be rejected rather than committed. **All three Scratchy Editorial Poster previews were checked against this at full resolution and none of them spells anything**: every background capital is cut by the frame or by the subject, and no complete word resolves in any of the three. The cover furniture the style bible names — illegible ruled mock body copy, rules, a folio mark, handwritten margin notes and arrows — is present, and where a margin note is legible it is recorded in that file's row rather than repaired.
