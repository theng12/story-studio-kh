# Backyard Naturalist preview provenance

Seven previews are declared by two of this collection's five standalone Content Packs. **None of them exists yet.** This file records why, exactly what must be run to produce them, and the prompt text prepared for each, so the render is a keyed operator's single step rather than a re-authoring pass.

The three packs that declare no raster assets — `com.storystudio.topic.backyard-creatures`, `com.storystudio.topic.backyard-plants` and `com.storystudio.metadata-formula.backyard-sleep-titles` — are complete: they build deterministically, sign, and pass `validateContentPack` under an ephemeral authoring key. The other two are **blocked on previews only**; their documents already validate against the signed Content Pack schema, which was checked directly by building each of them once in a throwaway directory under `/tmp` with disposable rasters in place. Those rasters were deleted and no placeholder byte was written into this repository.

## Three previews per visual style

The owner's rule, set on 2026-09-08 and applied to Royal History first, carries forward here: every Visual Style declares **three** previews rather than one, and the three are chosen so a buyer sees the style where it is easy, where it is quiet, and where it is hard.

- **hero** — the shot the style exists for, the one a cover would use.
- **scene** — a quiet wide page with **no creature in it at all**, so a buyer can see what the look does when nothing is moving. For a field-guide style whose subject is always one living thing, the honest empty case is a plant specimen alone on the paper: no animal, no insect, no eye anywhere in the frame.
- **face** — the hardest case the style can be asked for, chosen inside the style's own contract rather than against it.
  - Naturalist Sketchbook exists to build every value out of fine cross-hatching on bright cream paper, staying high-key throughout, with a light warm tint that never becomes the drawing. Its hardest case is therefore a small animal's face at close crop: at that range there is no silhouette to rescue the drawing, the paper still has to be the lightest thing in the picture, and every value on a curved, hairy, many-eyed head has to come from the spacing and crossing of pen strokes alone, with no wash to hide behind and no dark ground to sit against. If the look survives one face at that range, it survives everything else the recipe will ask of it.

The Thumbnail Recipe declares four, as the Lost Voyage, Royal History, Golden Epic, Midnight Archives, Sleep Theories and Gory Cartoon History recipes did, so that the fixed frame is shown against four different subjects and a buyer can see what actually varies between episodes. Two of the four carry a creature and two carry a plant, and the marker phrases run from two words to four, so a buyer can see the frame hold at both ends of its own rule.

## Why nothing was rendered in the authoring pass

`docs/VISUAL_STYLE_CONTENT_PACK_FORMAT.md` and the `story-studio-visual-style-adoption` skill both state that this control Mac verifies preview handoffs and does not perform production image generation, and that previews come only from owner-supplied original bytes or a production-preview handoff. That standing rule is lifted only by an explicit owner authorisation given in chat for one named pass, as it was for the earlier collections on 2026-09-08. No such authorisation covers this pass, so nothing was generated, nothing was substituted, and no placeholder bytes were written that would let a pack pass validation dishonestly.

## The exact command a keyed operator must run

Same route the earlier collections used: the Image Pool generator inside a running Story Studio app, in a project whose configured image product is the GenStudio text-to-image lane for `gpt-image-2`. Seven prompts, one variant each, aspect `16:9`, `reference_images: []` (text-only, no reference asset, no image-to-image, no compositing, no post-render typography — the marker phrase and the arrow in the recipe previews are drawn by the image model, as the recipe contract requires).

```
POST /api/projects/pack-previews/image-pool/generate
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "pool_id": "pack-previews",
  "model": "gpt-image-2",
  "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
  "resolution": "2K",
  "variants": 1,
  "run_id": "backyard-naturalist-previews-1",
  "prompts": [ ...the seven prompts below, in order... ]
}
```

Then poll the returned job, retain the returned PNGs outside the repository, and convert each accepted original with a whole-image resize (cover, lanczos3) plus WebP quality 90 effort 6 to the dimensions in the table — no crop beyond the sub-pixel aspect trim, no overlay, composite, or repair, falling back to lossless effort 6 for any file landing under the 20,480-byte floor. Record the original digest and byte count, the accepted digest, byte count and decode result, and the credits spent, in the table below before committing the bytes.

### Request size

These seven prompts serialise to **13,997 bytes**, which is inside the 16 KiB body cap that blocked the thirteen-prompt Midnight Archives batch on Story Studio 1.4.128 and earlier. Send them as one job with the run id above; the cap is why this collection's body is held to seven prompts per run id. Do not shorten, merge or reword a prompt to save bytes — the prompt text is the contract. The credits are 7 × 29 = 203.

## Acceptance rules specific to this collection

Original subjects only. Every creature and plant in these prompts is described from scratch as an ordinary member of a real species, and must be drawn from the description; a returned image that reproduces a recognisable character, a real person, a company mark, an invented insignia presented as genuine, or a copied composition must be rejected rather than committed. No human being, no hand and no bare skin appears in any prompt in this collection, and a returned image containing any of them must be rejected rather than committed. Anatomy is the product here: an image with the wrong number of legs, wings, eyes or leaflets for the species it names must be rejected rather than committed, however well it draws. Every subject must be alive and undamaged — an image showing a creature dead, pinned, squashed, injured or being killed must be rejected rather than committed. The look is high-key throughout: an image that arrives dim, lamplit, vignetted or set against a dark ground has broken the style's central promise and must be rejected rather than committed. In the three style previews no word may appear anywhere in the frame at all. In the four recipe previews the marker phrase must be spelled exactly as written, unclipped, in the stated number of lines, and it must be the only text in the picture; there is no scale bar, no label, no caption and no second phrase.

## Prompt 1 — `naturalist-sketchbook-hero.webp` (style preview, hero, no text)

> A naturalist's field-guide illustration in pen and ink on bright cream paper, sixteen by nine, drawn rather than rendered, and high-key throughout so the paper stays the lightest thing in the picture. One subject only: a single garden snail, whole, seen from slightly above and to the side, placed a little left of centre and low in the frame, at rest with its body extended and both pairs of tentacles up. Anatomy is scientifically accurate: a coiled shell of four and a half whorls turning to the right, a muscular foot with its fringed edge, a breathing pore on the right side of the mantle, two long upper tentacles carrying the eyes and two short lower ones. Contours are drawn once, confidently, with a fine nib in a thin dark brown-black ink of slightly living weight, a touch heavier where a form turns away and lighter where it turns into the light, and a few construction marks are left visible rather than erased. Every value is built from fine cross-hatching: parallel strokes at a shallow angle for the first tone, a second crossed set for the second, a third only in the deepest crevices, with the stroke direction following the surface — around the whorls of the shell, along the length of the foot. Nothing is smudged, blended, stippled into softness or filled solid black. Over the ink lies one light warm wash of thin honeyed ochre applied in a few broad transparent passes, warming the drawing without ever becoming it. The paper is bare cream everywhere else: more than half the frame carries nothing at all, the page is flat and frontal, and there is no horizon, no ground line, no scenery, no background object, no cast shadow and no vignette. Texture is the faint tooth of good cream paper and nothing else. No text, no lettering, no handwriting, no label, no scale bar, no numerals, no watermark, no signature, no border, no frame, no logo, no second creature, no human figure, no hand, no bare skin, no recognisable character, no dark ground, no lamplight, no injury.
