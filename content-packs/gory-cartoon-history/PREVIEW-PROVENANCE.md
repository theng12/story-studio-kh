# Gory Cartoon History preview provenance

Seven previews are declared by two of this collection's four standalone Content Packs. **None of them exist yet.** This file records why, exactly what must be run to produce them, and the prompt text prepared for each, so the render is a keyed operator's single step rather than a re-authoring pass.

The two packs that declare no raster assets — `com.storystudio.topic.obscure-history-horrors` and `com.storystudio.metadata-formula.obscure-history-listicle-titles` — are complete: they build deterministically, sign, and pass `validateContentPack` under an ephemeral authoring key. The other two are **blocked on previews only**; their documents already validate against the signed Content Pack schema, which was checked directly by building each of them once in a throwaway directory with disposable rasters in place. Those rasters were deleted and no placeholder byte was written into this repository.

## Three previews per visual style

The owner's rule, set on 2026-09-08 and applied to Royal History first, carries forward here: every Visual Style declares **three** previews rather than one, and the three are chosen so a buyer sees the style where it is easy, where it is quiet, and where it is hard.

- **hero** — the shot the style exists for, the one a cover would use.
- **scene** — a quiet wide scene with **no figure in it at all**, so a buyer can see what the look does to an empty room.
- **face** — the hardest case the style can be asked for, chosen inside the style's own contract rather than against it.
  - Bold Gory Cartoon exists to carry a whole picture on a thick outline, a flat fill and one hard shadow, so its hardest case is a single face held close at the top of its expression range: at that size there is no silhouette to rescue the drawing, no backdrop to separate it, and no gradient anywhere to model a cheek. If the look survives one face, it survives everything else the recipe will ask of it.

The Thumbnail Recipe declares four, as the Lost Voyage, Royal History, Golden Epic, Midnight Archives and Sleep Theories recipes did, so that the fixed frame is shown against four different lists and a buyer can see what actually varies between episodes. Two of the four show the base variant, with the panels running to the bottom edge, and two show the Timeline Bar variant, so both are shown twice rather than one being inferred from the other.

## Why nothing was rendered in this pass

`docs/VISUAL_STYLE_CONTENT_PACK_FORMAT.md` and the `story-studio-visual-style-adoption` skill both state that this control Mac verifies preview handoffs and does not perform production image generation, and that previews come only from owner-supplied original bytes or a production-preview handoff. Earlier passes rendered their previews under explicit owner authorisations given in chat for those passes only. No such authorisation exists for this pass, so nothing was generated, nothing was substituted, and no placeholder bytes were written that would let a pack pass validation dishonestly.

## The exact command a keyed operator must run

Same route the earlier collections used: the Image Pool generator inside a running Story Studio app, in a project whose configured image product is the GenStudio text-to-image lane for `gpt-image-2`. Seven prompts, one variant each, aspect `16:9`, `reference_images: []` (text-only, no reference asset, no image-to-image, no compositing, no post-render typography — the banner and the era labels are drawn by the image model, as the recipe contract requires).

```
POST /api/projects/<project-slug>/image-pool/generate
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "pool_id": "<image pool id>",
  "model": "<published GenStudio gpt-image-2 product id>",
  "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
  "resolution": "<published output row, 1K or higher>",
  "variants": 1,
  "run_id": "gory-cartoon-history-previews-1",
  "prompts": [ ...the seven prompts below, in order... ]
}
```

Then poll the returned job, retain the returned PNGs outside the repository, and convert each accepted original with a whole-image resize plus lossless WebP (effort 6) to the dimensions in the table — no crop, overlay, composite, or repair. Record the original digest and byte count, the accepted digest, byte count and decode result, and the credits spent, in the table below before committing the bytes.

### Request size

These seven prompts serialise to **11,985 bytes**, which is inside the 16 KiB body cap that blocked the thirteen-prompt Midnight Archives batch on Story Studio 1.4.128 and earlier. Send them as one job with the run id above; the cap is why this collection's bodies are held to seven prompts per run id. Do not shorten, merge or reword a prompt to save bytes — the prompt text is the contract. The credits are 7 × 29 = 203.

## Acceptance rules specific to this collection

Original subjects only. Every figure, creature and object in these prompts is described from scratch and must be drawn from the description; a returned image that reproduces a recognisable character, a real person, a company mark, an invented insignia presented as genuine, or a copied composition must be rejected rather than committed. The gore in this style is a cartoon prop and must read as one: bright flat red, clean rounded splats, smooth white bones with rounded ends, and no wet tissue, no anatomical detail and no figure in real distress. An image that reads as an injury rather than as a joke must be rejected rather than committed. In the four recipe previews the banner line and the era labels must be spelled exactly as written, unclipped at both ends and on one line; no other word may appear anywhere in the frame.

## Prompt 1 — `bold-gory-cartoon-hero.webp` (style preview, hero, no text)

> A loud printed-comic cartoon illustration, sixteen by nine, drawn rather than rendered. One subject only: a stout bearded warrior in a dented round helmet stands centred and slightly right, seen from the knees up, holding a battered axe in one hand and staring straight out of the frame with enormous round eyes, tiny pupils and a mouth open far wider than a jaw allows, showing a black interior, a flat pink tongue and simple square teeth. Every shape in the picture is closed by a thick black outline of even confident weight, heaviest on the outer silhouette, one step lighter inside the form, lighter again on the small details. All colour is flat and fully saturated with one hue per surface — pillar-box red, sun yellow, cadmium orange, hard cyan, bone white, black — and a single hard-edged cel shadow per object in a darker flat version of the same hue, thrown consistently from the upper left. A clean rounded splat of bright flat red sits on the axe head and three thick outlined drips run from it, drawn as smooth cartoon shapes with no texture and no wetness. Two small flat white bone shapes with rounded ends lie on the ground beside his boot like props. The backdrop is a single flat field of deep teal with nothing in it at all, a clear step away in hue and value from the figure so the silhouette pops. Sweat beads fly off the helmet as small flat teardrops and short straight motion lines radiate from the head. No gradient, no airbrush, no blur, no texture, no rendered highlight, no perspective haze. Camera level, generous flat ground around the figure. No text, no lettering, no numerals, no watermark, no signature, no speech balloon, no panel border, no logo, no second figure, no recognisable individual, no realistic injury, no wet tissue, no anatomical detail.
