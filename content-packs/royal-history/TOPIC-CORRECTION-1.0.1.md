- **`Qing emperors`, tuple 14 (Dorgon):** use **1651**, not 1650, for the posthumous condemnation. Dorgon died in December 1650; the posthumous denunciation decree was issued 12 March 1651. *Eminent Chinese of the Ch'ing Period*, public-domain biographical reference, [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Eminent_Chinese_of_the_Ch%27ing_Period/Dorgon). - **`Qing emperors`, tuple 13 (Prince Gong):** the Convention of Peking was 1860; the Zongli Yamen was established in **1861**. The amended scope retains both steps. Columbia’s late-Qing political timeline places the Convention in October 1860 and the Yamen’s establishment in February 1861: [Columbia University](https://www.columbia.edu/~gas2122/political_timeline.html). - **`Achaemenid kings of Persia`, tuple 19 (Artabanus):** Artabanus was Xerxes' guard commander and the assassination was in August 465 BCE; the wording stays at palace-guard coup rather than claiming an unsupported exclusive site. [Encyclopaedia Iranica, “Artabanus”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/artabanus-achaemenid/). [Met, “Idia, First Queen Mother of Benin”](https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/essays/idia-the-first-queen-mother-of-benin).

## Follow-up corrections

- **`Qing emperors`, tuple 17 (Prince Chun):** this is **Yixuan**, father of the Guangxu Emperor, not Zaifeng, father of the Xuantong Emperor. A translated 1875 Qing decree identifies Zaitian, son of Prince Chun (Yixuan), as adopted into the Xianfeng line: [U.S. Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1875v01/d145). The scope is therefore `Beijing court at Guangxu's succession in 1875`, not 1908.
- **`Bourbon kings of France`, tuple 19 (Louis, Duke of Burgundy):** remove the scope's claimed smallpox cause. The useful factual unit is the **1711–1712 succession crisis**; the Grand Dauphin died in 1711, while the Duke and Duchess of Burgundy died in February 1712 and their elder son followed weeks later. [Wikisource’s *Louis XIV et son siècle* chronology](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Louis_XIV_et_son_si%C3%A8cle/Chapitre_LI). The optional replacement mechanism does not assert a shared disease or a cause of death.
- **`Song emperors`, tuple 17 (Yingzong):** `Puyang` was a mistaken anglicisation. The issue was the **Prince of Pu** (`Puyi`) rites debate over honors for Yingzong's biological father. [Chinese Text Project, *Songshi Jishi Benmo*, “Pu Debate”](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=725892&if=en&remap=gb); a modern academic abstract also identifies it as the debate over Yingzong’s biological father, Prince of Pu: [Taiwan Academic Journals](https://toaj.stpi.niar.org.tw/index/journal/volume/article/4b1141f989027565018913ff6efe221b).
