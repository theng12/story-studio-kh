# Sleep Theories preview provenance

Seven previews are declared by two of this collection's three standalone Content Packs. **None of them exist yet.** This file records why, exactly what must be run to produce them, and the prompt text prepared for each, so the render is a keyed operator's single step rather than a re-authoring pass.

The pack that declares no raster assets — `com.storystudio.topic.mysteries-and-cover-ups` — is complete: it builds deterministically, signs, and passes `validateContentPack` under an ephemeral authoring key. The other two are **blocked on previews only**; their documents already validate against the signed Content Pack schema, which was checked directly by building each of them once in a throwaway directory with disposable rasters in place. Those rasters were deleted and no placeholder byte was written into this repository.

## Three previews per visual style

The owner's rule, set on 2026-09-08 and applied to Royal History first, carries forward here: every Visual Style declares **three** previews rather than one, and the three are chosen so a buyer sees the style where it is easy, where it is quiet, and where it is hard.

- **hero** — the shot the style exists for, the one a cover would use.
- **scene** — a quiet wide scene with **no figure in it at all**, so a buyer can see what the look does to an empty room.
- **face** — the hardest case the style can be asked for, chosen inside the style's own contract rather than against it.
  - Comic Pulp Mystery exists to put one glowing object in a wet night and light everything from it, so its hardest case is a single face held close and carried entirely on that one coloured light: flat cel shading has two steps and a hard boundary to work with, the rain and the halftone are still on the frame at close range, and there is no fill anywhere to rescue the shadow side.

The Thumbnail Recipe declares four, as the Lost Voyage, Royal History, Golden Epic and Midnight Archives recipes did, so that the fixed frame is shown against four different objects, settings and banner lines and a buyer can see what actually varies between episodes.

## Why nothing was rendered in this pass

`docs/VISUAL_STYLE_CONTENT_PACK_FORMAT.md` and the `story-studio-visual-style-adoption` skill both state that this control Mac verifies preview handoffs and does not perform production image generation, and that previews come only from owner-supplied original bytes or a production-preview handoff. The 2026-09-06 sleep-history pass rendered its six previews under an explicit owner authorisation given in chat for that pass only. No such authorisation exists for this pass, so nothing was generated, nothing was substituted, and no placeholder bytes were written that would let a pack pass validation dishonestly.

## The exact command a keyed operator must run

Same route the sleep-history previews used: the Image Pool generator inside a running Story Studio app, in a project whose configured image product is the GenStudio text-to-image lane for `gpt-image-2`. Seven prompts, one variant each, aspect `16:9`, `reference_images: []` (text-only, no reference asset, no image-to-image, no compositing, no post-render typography — the banner and tag text is drawn by the image model, as the recipe contract requires).

```
POST /api/projects/<project-slug>/image-pool/generate
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "pool_id": "<image pool id>",
  "model": "<published GenStudio gpt-image-2 product id>",
  "aspect_ratio": "16:9",
  "resolution": "<published output row, 1K or higher>",
  "variants": 1,
  "run_id": "sleep-theories-previews-1",
  "prompts": [ ...the seven prompts below, in order... ]
}
```

Then poll the returned job, retain the returned PNGs outside the repository, and convert each accepted original with a whole-image resize plus lossless WebP (effort 6) to the dimensions in the table — no crop, overlay, composite, or repair. Record the original digest and byte count, the accepted digest, byte count and decode result, and the credits spent, in the table below before committing the bytes.

### Request size

These seven prompts serialise to **12,328 bytes**, which is inside the 16 KiB body cap that blocked the thirteen-prompt Midnight Archives batch on Story Studio 1.4.128 and earlier. Send them as one job with the run id above. Do not shorten, merge or reword a prompt to save bytes — the prompt text is the contract. The credits are 7 × 29 = 203.

## Prompt 1 — `comic-pulp-mystery-hero.webp` (style preview, hero, no text)

> A graphic-novel illustration in the register of a pulp paperback cover, sixteen by nine, drawn rather than photographed. A wet street at night: every form is closed by a heavy black outline of confident, slightly uneven weight, thickest on the silhouettes and thinning where a surface turns into the light, and every surface is shaded with flat cel steps that meet on hard edges, never blended. Standing on the ground at the left of the frame, an old cylindrical metal canister with a dented lid lies open and is burning with a hot orange glow that is the only light in the picture; it throws long hard-edged shadows away from itself across the flooded tarmac and up the walls. Everything the glow reaches is orange and amber; everything else — the sky, the wet road, the glass, the brick, the whole right side of the frame — is a cold saturated teal going to dense blue-black, with no muddy middle where the two meet. Rain falls as long straight pale-teal streaks at a constant angle across the entire frame and breaks into small hard splash marks on the road; the standing water returns the glow as a broken vertical smear. Behind the rooftops, one flat white-blue lightning shape silhouettes the skyline and leaves everything in front of it black. An even halftone dot screen lies over the flat colour with fine print grain and a faint misregistration on one channel. Low, slightly tilted camera with strong diagonals; the upper right of the frame is quiet dark ground. No figure, no face, no text, no lettering, no watermark, no signature, no speech balloon, no panel border, no logo, no modern brand marking.

## Prompt 3 — `comic-pulp-mystery-face.webp` (style preview, hardest case, no text)

> A graphic-novel illustration pushed to the hardest case this look can be asked for, sixteen by nine. One anonymous face fills the right half of the frame, cut at the shoulders, held very close, wet with rain and caught in the moment of recognising something: the eyes wide, the mouth just open, water running from the hair down the temple and along the jaw. The only light in the picture is a hot orange glow coming from an unseen source low at the left, so the face is carried entirely on that one coloured light with no fill, no second key and no rim from behind. Everything is still drawn the same way at this range: a heavy black outline of uneven weight closing every form, flat cel shading with exactly two steps per surface and one hard black shadow shape, no blending anywhere, and the shadow side of the face resolved in cold saturated teal rather than in grey. Each rain drop on the skin is a small hard-edged shape with a single orange highlight, not a soft render. Rain falls in long straight pale-teal streaks across the whole frame including across the face; one flat white-blue lightning shape fires behind the head and leaves the silhouette black. An even halftone dot screen and print grain lie over the entire image at the same weight as at a distance, with a faint misregistration on one channel. The left third of the frame is dense blue-black ground. No softening of the shadowed side, no glow bloom over the image, no second light, no text, no lettering, no watermark, no signature, no speech balloon, no panel border, no second face, no recognisable individual.
