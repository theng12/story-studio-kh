# Semantic guardrails for Topic Pack Tasks 3-17

**Purpose:** Read-only planning aid for implementers and independent reviewers. It covers all fifteen remaining receipt-bound `1.0.1` identities after Task 2. It does not alter the approved design, plan, schema, registry, manifests, runtime, or merchandising contract.

**Source basis:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-25-topic-pack-1000-repair-design.md`, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-25-topic-pack-1000-repair.md`, the Task 2 Natural World Animals review, each predecessor Topic Pack, and its compatible Formula.

## Batch-wide hard stops

- **Author profile tuples atomically.** The episode object, time/place scope, mechanism, and evidence lens must describe one coherent episode treatment. Do not build 20 siblings by rotating four global ordinal lists or by adding the subject name to generic strings.
- **A scope must locate the actual event.** Reject a scope that is merely grammatical, broadly thematic, temporally impossible, or incompatible with the object/mechanism. The Task 2 failure pattern was a small repeated scope template attached to unrelated events.
- **An evidence lens must be capable of testing the paired claim.** It must name a defensible record, observation, measurement, proxy, or source type for that exact object/mechanism. Reject generic methods reused at the same tuple ordinal, invented archives, and evidence that could not bear the proposed inference.
- **Structural uniqueness is necessary but insufficient.** Distinct strings, fingerprints, objects, and mechanisms do not rescue synonyms, template substitutions, repeated story beats, or semantically duplicate core questions.
- **Filmability is part of usefulness.** Each treatment needs at least one legible person, artifact, document, place, process, measurement, or material trace. Reject abstract noun piles that cannot support scenes, maps, diagrams, archival B-roll, or grounded reenactment.
- **Factual posture must match the source.** Separate documented record, observation/proxy, reasonable inference, contested interpretation, and speculation. Never invent quotations, citations, consensus, intent, diagnoses, outcomes, or access to records.
- **Safety and dignity remain tuple-level gates.** A globally safe prompt does not excuse a tuple that glamorizes coercion, gives professional advice, diagnoses a viewer, stereotypes a people, turns suffering into spectacle, or supplies unsafe operational instruction.
- **Profile review means 1,000 actual verdicts.** Review each subject's 20 tuples for object/scope/mechanism/evidence fit; do not certify a generated template family on representative samples alone.
- **Independent-axis review means universality, not vagueness.** For Tasks 12-17, every one of the 20 material values in each axis must create a useful, coherent treatment with every one of the 50 subjects. A value is not universal merely because it can be appended grammatically. If specificity breaks universality, narrow the subjects or use an existing-schema coverage group/profile; do not blur the value.
- **Keep temporary SDD evidence ignored.** Semantic reports and universal matrices remain available on disk for Task 19 evidence copying/hash binding, but are not force-added to the repository index.

## Task 3 — Human Behavior Social Dynamics

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.human-behavior-social-dynamics` / `com.storystudio.topic.human-behavior-social-dynamics` (profile; 50 subjects × 20 tuples).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Reusing the same conversation, workplace, relationship, or social-media scenarios under different labels.
- Inferring intent, personality disorder, attachment diagnosis, or manipulation from one ambiguous behavior.
- Pop-psychology catchphrases, “dark truth” framing, tactical manipulation advice, or a healthy-response field disguised as certainty.
- Evidence lenses that name “a study,” self-report, or conversation coding without being able to distinguish the proposed mechanism from alternatives.
- Scopes that erase the relationship, power difference, duration, or sequence needed to interpret the behavior.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Start from an observable sequence, identify plausible competing explanations, and keep mechanism claims proportional to the evidence.
- Make the relationship/context material: peer versus manager, new team versus long relationship, public versus private, one event versus repeated pattern.
- Require a claim-capable method for each tuple: timestamped versions for changing terms, coded interaction sequences for turn-taking, longitudinal observations for repair, and so on.
- Preserve agency and non-diagnostic language; a tuple may explain a pattern or trade-off, never declare what the viewer or another person “is.”
- Confirm the 20 siblings for a subject investigate materially different signals or decisions, not twenty phrasings of refusal, silence, status, or trust.

## Task 4 — Night Journeys

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.night-journeys` / `com.storystudio.topic.night-journeys` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Fifty “cozy place + gentle chore” subjects whose 20 siblings differ only by prop, color, weather, or time of night.
- Quiet-looking tasks that introduce urgency, fire/water/fall risk, isolation anxiety, countdowns, unresolved mystery, loss, or a threatening antagonist.
- A scope that changes the place or character so much that the sibling is no longer the selected journey.
- Repeating “slow walking,” “gentle sorting,” “sensory continuity,” and “completed small task” as generic ordinal slots.
- Beautiful but unfilmable abstractions with no continuous task, route, or settled ending.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Each tuple must retain one quiet character, one stable setting, one soothing through-line, and one completed low-stakes task or journey.
- The mechanism is the actual calm procedure—mending a seam, trimming a wick, logging a shell—not a mood adjective.
- Check the full emotional arc for low arousal: no hook, challenge, surprise reveal, urgency, cliffhanger, CTA, or unresolved question.
- Require distinct material actions and sensory environments within each profile while preserving continuity and safe, plausible procedure.
- Evidence lenses here are production/narrative constraints rather than scientific proof; they must still match the tuple (for example, ceramic-and-water sound detail only where ceramic and water are present).

## Task 5 — Habits and Momentum

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.habits-momentum` / `com.storystudio.topic.habits-momentum` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Generic “cue design,” “friction reduction,” note-card, calendar, timer, bottle, or checklist substitutions across unrelated routines.
- Hustle, shame, domination, “no excuses,” or guaranteed productivity/income/identity outcomes.
- Medical, nutrition, hydration, exercise, or mental-health advice presented as universally safe.
- Experiments that are not bounded, reversible, measurable, accessible, or compatible with rest and real constraints.
- Treating adherence as success while ignoring burden, accessibility, opportunity cost, or the option to decline.

**Acceptance lenses**

- One concrete friction, one specific environmental/planning change, one bounded observation window, and one honest trade-off per tuple.
- The object must implement the named mechanism in the stated moment; a calendar is not automatically temporal cueing, and a bottle is not automatically progress feedback.
- Evidence must measure the intended friction or start behavior without inflating it into a life outcome.
- Include constraint-sensitive and accessible alternatives; preserve rest and non-participation as legitimate outcomes.
- Permit only the documented `a water bottle|cue design` reuse if the current source test still explicitly allows it; otherwise core pairs remain unique.

## Task 6 — Everyday Calm

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.everyday-calm` / `com.storystudio.topic.everyday-calm` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- The same breathing, five-senses, gaze, labeling, or body-scan practice pasted into every setting.
- Clinical claims (“reduces anxiety,” “regulates the nervous system,” “improves sleep”) or implied treatment certainty.
- Physical instructions that may be unsuitable, inaccessible, painful, unsafe while walking/driving/cooking, or hard to stop.
- Turning calm into another productivity requirement or framing discomfort as failure.
- Generic evidence labels such as “sensory specificity” that do not identify what the listener actually notices or chooses.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Keep every practice optional, ordinary, non-medical, easy to stop, and adaptable without penalty.
- Tie the object, posture, environment, and mechanism together; reject instructions incompatible with the stated activity or location.
- Make the sensory anchor concrete and filmable, and distinguish noticing from claims about psychological or physiological outcomes.
- Include comfort/accessibility choice wherever body movement, breath pacing, touch, balance, vision, or sound is involved.
- Ensure 20 siblings vary the actual practice and moment, not only the household prop or sensory adjective.

## Task 7 — Ancient Humans Survival

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.ancient-humans-survival` / `com.storystudio.topic.ancient-humans-survival` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Anachronistic tools, materials, species, climates, settlement patterns, medicine, or social institutions.
- Generic survival beats—fire, spear, hide, tracks, hearth—reassigned without period/site compatibility.
- Presenting ethnographic analogy, experimental archaeology, skeletal inference, or contested reconstruction as direct proof.
- Caveman comedy, universal claims about “ancient humans,” unsupported gender roles, or dehumanizing treatment of injury, childbirth, famine, and death.
- Evidence lenses that cannot support the specific behavior claimed, especially intent, cooperation, treatment, or route knowledge.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Bind each tuple to a plausible hominin population/period/environment where needed, with a concrete artifact, feature, remain, or landscape trace.
- State what the evidence directly shows, what the mechanism reasonably infers, and where analogy or uncertainty enters.
- Check tool/material/species and environmental chronology; reject a vivid scene whose components never coexisted.
- Keep survival solutions practical and human without turning them into modern how-to instructions or fantasy certainty.
- Require 20 materially distinct adaptations per subject, not variants of one hearth/shelter/tool solution.

## Task 8 — Historical Wars, Treaties, and World Order (History cut)

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.historical-wars-treaties-world-order` / `com.storystudio.topic.historical-wars-treaties-world-order` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Battlefield spectacle, weapon trivia, casualty voyeurism, or a commander biography replacing causation.
- A treaty, assassination, leader, ethnic group, or ideology made the sole cause of a conflict or later order.
- Objects/scopes borrowed across wars, negotiations, theaters, or phases where they did not exist.
- Evidence labels that invent an archive or cannot support the proposed diplomatic, logistical, civilian, or political mechanism.
- Duplicating Task 17's Archival Ledger systemic investigations instead of preserving a distinct History Explained treatment.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Each tuple needs a bounded event/phase, concrete record or material trace, mechanism, and consequence; keep objectives, resources, alliances, logistics, diplomacy, and civilians visible.
- Check dates, borders, institutions, technologies, participants, and document provenance at tuple level.
- Treat atrocity, occupation, forced movement, and civilian harm with factual restraint and human dignity.
- Use evidence capable of the claim: treaty clauses for negotiated obligations, logistics records for supply, civilian records for home-front capacity—not interchangeable “archives.”
- Keep the History cut event-and-consequence oriented; reserve Archival Ledger's nine-step, multi-causal institutional reconstruction for Task 17.

## Task 9 — POV Historical Occupations

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.pov-historical-occupations` / `com.storystudio.topic.pov-historical-occupations` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Costume-tour roleplay, generic “day in the life,” invented inner monologue, or unsupported second-person certainty.
- Combining duties, tools, ranks, legal status, and rewards from different centuries, places, or institutions.
- Glamourizing violence, enslavement, coercion, exploitation, caste, child labor, or lethal working conditions.
- Repeating training/routine/danger/reward as labels without a distinct object and scene for each.
- Treating a diverse occupation as one universal experience and omitting gender, class, legal status, or regional variation where material.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Name the polity/place, bounded period, institutional setting, and status distinction needed for the tuple to be true.
- Across 20 siblings, cover materially distinct lifecycle stages: entry, training, routine, hierarchy, pay/provisioning, risk, advancement, discipline, exit, and aftermath.
- Anchor “you” narration in records and material detail; qualify what is typical, possible, exceptional, or unknown.
- Give coercive and dangerous roles contextual dignity without thrill-seeking detail or actionable violence.
- Ensure each tuple has a filmable tool, document, workplace, task, or trace whose evidence lens genuinely supports the role mechanism.

## Task 10 — Deep Time Earth Systems

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.deep-time-earth-systems` / `com.storystudio.topic.deep-time-earth-systems` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- One “magic cause,” teleology, or a clean linear story for an interacting Earth system.
- Swapping the same zircon, isotope, core, layer, or model across processes where the proxy is irrelevant.
- Timescale errors, impossible co-occurrences, outdated reconstructions stated as consensus, or local evidence inflated to global certainty.
- Abstract process labels with no observable trace, location, time interval, or uncertainty.
- Confusing measurement/proxy with the mechanism it helps infer.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Every tuple binds a material trace or measurement, a named/bounded geological setting and interval, a physical mechanism, and a claim-capable evidence method.
- Verify chronology, scale, preservation limits, proxy calibration, and whether the claim is local, regional, or global.
- Make interacting feedbacks and alternative interpretations visible; distinguish established process, active debate, and speculation.
- Keep objects visually legible—rock contact, core, fossil, landform, map, instrument output—so a scene can explain how evidence becomes inference.
- Require 20 different causal/evidentiary treatments per subject, not 20 proxy-name substitutions around one narrative.

## Task 11 — Economics Businesses

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.economics-businesses` / `com.storystudio.topic.economics-businesses` (profile; 50 × 20).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Applying generic utilization, margin, churn, cash-flow, or bottleneck templates to businesses whose actual operating model differs.
- Invented revenue/cost numbers, unlabelled representative assumptions, private-company claims without evidence, or present-day figures detached from place/currency/period.
- Profit promises, investment/personal-finance advice, “secret business model” framing, or labor/regulatory costs treated as noise.
- Confusing a company with an industry archetype, or using one famous company's facts as universal for the category.
- Objects and evidence that cannot measure the named economic mechanism.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Each tuple opens the books on a concrete operating moment with place, scale, currency, and period where quantitative claims need them.
- Connect the physical/documentary object to revenue, cost, capacity, labor, cash flow, risk, or trade-off; do not merely name a metric.
- Label observed figures, public disclosures, regulatory data, operator records, and representative scenarios honestly.
- Include labor, regulation, working capital, seasonality, failure modes, and externalities where they materially shape the model.
- Make 20 siblings distinct operating questions across the business lifecycle rather than twenty dashboard metrics on the same throughput story.

## Task 12 — Audience Question Seeder

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.audience-question-seeder` / `com.storystudio.topic.audience-question-seeder` (independent; 50 subjects; ≥20 universal values per material axis).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Expanding 50 “subjects” into synonymous question intents rather than distinct, reusable question domains.
- Universal values made so vague (“an example,” “a context,” “consider evidence”) that they create no concrete or filmable episode.
- Actionability that becomes diagnosis, treatment, legal conclusion, financial instruction, unsafe experiment, live directory, or one-size-fits-all advice.
- Evidence types paired with conclusions they cannot support, especially anecdote used for causality or universal claims.
- Taking over the Question Ladder's answer structure, becoming UI seed data, or duplicating normalized questions.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Each subject/value combination must yield one plain-language, answerable, bounded question with concrete nouns and a real evidence path.
- Test all 50 subjects against every audience-context/object, constraint/scope, actionability/mechanism, and evidence-lens value; each must remain safe and meaningful without hidden substitutions.
- Keep the Topic Pack to question domains; the Formula alone owns mechanism → trade-off → exception → bounded conclusion structure.
- A reversible experiment must be low-risk for every subject or it is not universal. “Ask a professional” must mark a boundary, not fabricate access or a referral.
- Run semantic duplicate checks after normalization; reject synonym ladders and questions differing only by audience adjective or constraint word.

## Task 13 — Archival Ledger: Empires, Trade, and Extraction

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.archival-ledger.empires-trade-extraction` / `com.storystudio.topic.archival-ledger.empires-trade-extraction` (independent; 50; ≥20 universal values per material axis).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Calling chartered-company rule, settler expansion, plantation labor, or maritime chokepoints “universal” across subjects where they do not apply.
- Making axis values abstract enough to pass grammar while losing an identifiable document, transaction, labor relation, route, institution, or place.
- Single-villain colonial history, economics-only explanation, civilizational ranking, ethnic/religious scapegoating, or erasing local and worker agency.
- Anachronistic categories, teleological “decline,” or applying one empire's institutions to another.
- Evidence that privileges official/company archives while claiming the experience or intent of colonized communities without testimony or local scholarship.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Subject eligibility is conditional on all 20 values per material axis being genuinely supportable; remove a subject rather than dilute a failed value.
- Each combination must trace a concrete relation among power, trade, extraction, labor, commodity flow, and resistance, with distributed agency.
- Require source plurality appropriate to the claim: administrative/company/shipping records plus labor testimony, local histories, material traces, or transnational comparison where needed.
- Name place, period, institution, affected community, and contested inference precisely; never infer private intent from outcome alone.
- Verify universality row by row and reject “universal” terms that are really mechanisms specific to only a subset of empires or trade systems.

## Task 14 — Archival Ledger: Infrastructure, Commodities, and Corporate Power

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.archival-ledger.infrastructure-commodities-corporate-power` / `com.storystudio.topic.archival-ledger.infrastructure-commodities-corporate-power` (independent; 50; ≥20 universal values per material axis).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Technology as autonomous force, inevitable progress, or a corporation acting outside states, finance, labor, regulation, and public choices.
- Modern network/corporate concepts retrojected onto ancient or early-modern systems.
- “Universal” objects such as stock certificates, engineering plans, grids, or corporate archives that many subjects cannot have.
- Celebratory infrastructure spectacle that omits displacement, labor, health, environmental cost, access, or unequal control.
- Commodity, engineering, and public-health evidence used interchangeably regardless of the mechanism claimed.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Every combination must identify what capacity the system enabled, who financed/controlled it, which bottleneck or incentive changed, and who bore cost or exclusion.
- Universal values must be era-neutral yet concrete—material trace, route/capacity record, rule, transaction, maintenance burden—not modern jargon stripped of period.
- Bind engineering records to physical capability, corporate/financial records to control and funding, labor/public-health evidence to human cost, and planning records to institutional choice.
- Distinguish network construction, operation, maintenance, financing, concentration, dependency, and consequence as separate causal treatments.
- Reject progress-only and villain-only narratives; preserve institutions, alternatives, contingency, and distributional effects.

## Task 15 — Archival Ledger: Money, Debt, and Financial Institutions

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.archival-ledger.money-debt-financial-institutions` / `com.storystudio.topic.archival-ledger.money-debt-financial-institutions` (independent; 50; ≥20 universal values per material axis).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Modern personal-finance/investment advice, market prediction, guaranteed lessons, or present-day directives.
- One bank, family, central banker, creditor, or conspiracy made omnipotent without evidence.
- Anachronistic use of modern balance-sheet, monetary-policy, liquidity, or capital-market concepts.
- “Universal” bank-run, central-bank, currency-convertibility, or household-credit values applied to subjects without those institutions.
- Nominal figures, price series, debt terms, or policy documents cited without currency regime, period, scale, or source limitations.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Test universality using institution- and era-correct language; if a value presupposes a central bank, public bond market, or modern accounting, it is not universal.
- Each combination connects a concrete instrument/record/regime to money, credit, debt, taxation, prices, employment, state capacity, or ordinary households through a bounded mechanism.
- Distinguish solvency, liquidity, inflation, exchange regime, fiscal choice, leverage, capital flow, and settlement rather than collapsing all into “financial crisis.”
- Use documented records and proportional causal claims; disclose disputed series, missing data, and interpretive uncertainty.
- Preserve explanatory history, never actionable financial advice or claims that one institution secretly controls every outcome.

## Task 16 — Archival Ledger: States, Revolutions, and Collapse

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.archival-ledger.states-revolutions-collapse` / `com.storystudio.topic.archival-ledger.states-revolutions-collapse` (independent; 50; ≥20 universal values per material axis).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Hero biography, inevitable national character, unsupported conspiracy, or one ideological slogan as the whole explanation.
- Treating reform, revolution, civil war, secession, independence, reunification, and state collapse as interchangeable processes.
- Universal values that assume a constitution, mass party, capital/province structure, modern bureaucracy, or national state where none existed.
- Scapegoating ethnic/religious groups, flattening contested identities, or turning state violence into spectacle or tactical instruction.
- Retrospective certainty about actors' intent and a clean “before/after” that erases regional variation and failed alternatives.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Each combination must distinguish background condition, institutional pressure, coalition/actor choice, immediate trigger, feedback, and consequence.
- Use polity- and period-correct institutions; name whose legitimacy, administration, fiscal system, coalition, or command structure changed.
- Require evidence appropriate to the claim and group: constitutional/administrative texts, correspondence, demographic/material evidence, testimony, and historiographical debate.
- Test every universal value against every subject for real applicability, not metaphorical resemblance.
- Preserve contingency, competing interpretations, human cost, and agency without declaring whole peoples inherently revolutionary, violent, or incapable of government.

## Task 17 — Archival Ledger: Wars, Treaties, and World Order

**Identity:** `com.storystudio.pack.topic.archival-ledger.wars-treaties-world-order` / `com.storystudio.topic.archival-ledger.wars-treaties-world-order` (independent; 50; ≥20 universal values per material axis).

**Most likely failure modes**

- Copying Task 8's profile subjects/objects/treatments into a second identity instead of authoring the Archival Ledger causal-systems cut.
- Battlefield spectacle, military-technology determinism, treaty monocausality, or inevitable-war narratives.
- Universal values that assume a modern alliance, international organization, industrial home front, deterrence doctrine, or written treaty across all periods.
- Operational military detail that is tactically instructive rather than historically explanatory.
- Diplomatic or military archives used to claim civilian experience, occupied-community agency, private intent, or consensus they cannot establish.

**Acceptance lenses**

- Maintain identity separation: Task 8 explains bounded events and consequences for History formulas; Task 17 reconstructs multi-layer causation through institutions, material pressures, alliances, decisions, contingency, and evidence limits.
- Every combination must remain applicable across its curated subjects using period-correct language and a concrete document, logistical trace, negotiation, institution, or civilian record.
- Keep war aims, alliance commitments, resources/logistics, diplomacy, occupation, civilian capacity/cost, and settlement analytically distinct.
- Verify universality for premodern and modern subjects alike; reject values that rely on concepts or records absent in part of the corpus.
- Close on the best-supported causal picture and uncertainty, not a hidden-truth reveal, grand moral, or reusable tactical lesson.

## Reviewer completion checklist

For each Task 3-17 successor, the reviewer should be able to answer **yes** to all applicable items:

1. All 50 subjects are in-scope, distinct, evidence-supportable, and capable of 20 material siblings.
2. Every profile tuple or independent-axis combination is coherent as a whole, not only valid field by field.
3. No repeated ordinal template, generic substitution, synonym padding, title-only variation, or cross-pack identity bleed remains.
4. Every mechanism has a compatible scope and a claim-capable evidence lens; uncertainty is honest.
5. Every assignment supports concrete visual planning and a materially distinct episode treatment.
6. Exclusions and Formula compatibility hold at tuple/matrix level, including safety, dignity, professional boundaries, and non-sensational framing.
7. The semantic report (and universal matrix for Tasks 12-17) binds the exact successor document hash and records the full review, not a representative-sample pass.
8. Temporary review evidence remains ignored on disk for Task 19; it is not force-added to Git.
