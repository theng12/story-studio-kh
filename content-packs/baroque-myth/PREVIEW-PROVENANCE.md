# Baroque Myth preview provenance

Four previews are declared by two of this collection's three standalone Content Packs. All four now exist, rendered under the owner's authorisation of 2026-09-08. This file records why, exactly what must be run to produce them, and the prompt text prepared for each, so the render is a keyed operator's single step rather than a re-authoring pass.

The pack that declares no raster assets — `com.storystudio.topic.gods-heroes-and-epics` — is complete: it builds deterministically, signs, and passes `validateContentPack` under an ephemeral authoring key. The other two are **blocked on previews only**; their documents already validate against the signed Content Pack schema, which was checked directly by building each of them once in a throwaway directory with disposable rasters in place. Those rasters were deleted and no placeholder byte was written into this repository.

## Three previews per visual style, one swatch per lettering style

The owner's rule, set on 2026-09-08 and applied to Royal History first, carries forward here: every Visual Style declares **three** previews rather than one, and the three are chosen so a buyer sees the style where it is easy, where it is quiet, and where it is hard.

- **hero** — the shot the style exists for, the one a cover would use.
- **scene** — a quiet wide scene with **no figure in it at all**, so a buyer can see what the look does to an empty frame. This is the hard one for a style whose whole subject is figures on cloud, which is exactly why it is worth showing.
- **face** — the hardest case the style can be asked for, chosen inside the style's own contract rather than against it.
  - Baroque Mythological Sky refuses the close-cropped screaming face by name in its negative prompt, so its hardest case is the opposite: one calm head held very close, carried entirely on warm flesh against a stormy sky with a single high source and no fill, where the blended half-tones, the canvas tooth and the varnish bloom all have to survive at portrait range without any drapery, any diagonal or any cherub to carry the composition.

The Lettering Style declares one swatch, at the 1024x576 figure the shipped paid lettering packs already use, because a lettering style is bought by looking at it and one correctly spelled specimen is what there is to look at.

## Under what authorisation these were rendered

`docs/VISUAL_STYLE_CONTENT_PACK_FORMAT.md` and the `story-studio-visual-style-adoption` skill both state that this control Mac verifies preview handoffs and does not perform production image generation, and that previews come only from owner-supplied original bytes or a production-preview handoff. That standing rule is lifted only by an explicit owner authorisation given in chat for one named pass, as it was for the earlier collections. **This collection's previews were rendered under such an authorisation, given on 2026-09-08**, which named the model (`gpt-image-2` through GenStudio, no other route), set a credit ceiling, and instructed that the results be published without a further confirmation. Nothing was substituted and no placeholder byte was ever written to let a pack pass validation dishonestly: the run below is the only source of these four files.

## The safety refusal of 2026-09-08, and why prompts 1 and 3 were reworded

The four prompts were sent as one job (`run_id` `baroque-myth-previews-1`, job `a9788008`) against the published GenStudio `gpt-image-2` lane. Prompts 2 and 4 returned and were accepted. **Prompts 1 and 3 were refused by the provider's safety system before any image was made** — `IMAGE_CONTENT_REFUSED`, HTTP 400, `safety_violations=["sexual"]`, request ids `req_0fa425b257054860b57bf692db290992` (prompt 1) and `req_244b1afcf3f94d28b97b13df8d50a28f` (prompt 3). No bytes were returned and 29 credits were released for each.

Neither prompt asked for anything sexual. Both described a baroque figure in the ordinary art-historical way — drapery arranged over an otherwise undraped body, bare shoulders, a bare torso, winged infants — and that vocabulary is what the classifier scored. A refusal is not a transport error: resending the same text costs credits and fails again. So the wording was changed, and only the wording that caused the refusal:

- **Every figure is now fully draped in heavy robes** from neck to ankle, with only faces and hands uncovered. Drapery is still a character in the picture and still snaps and falls in crimson, gold and slate; it now covers a clothed body rather than framing an undraped one.
- **No bare torsos, no bare shoulders, no nudity of any kind**, stated positively in the body of each prompt and again in its negative tail.
- **Cherubs are omitted from the hero prompt** rather than clothed, which the owner's rule allows. Winged infants were the second half of the classifier's reading, and the collection's Visual Style still declares them, so the look loses nothing that a buyer is shown — the style bible keeps cherubs and now says plainly that they are clothed and may be left out.
- **No named painter.** Both prompts and the style bible now say "in the high-baroque manner" instead of naming an artist, per the owner's instruction of 2026-09-08.
- Everything else the look is bought for is untouched: gods on cloud banks, the long rising diagonal, crimson, gold and slate drapery, strong chiaroscuro from a single high source, the canvas tooth, the craquelure and the varnish bloom.

The style document changed with them, so the shipped contract and the preview that demonstrates it cannot drift apart: `content/visual-style.json` now describes fully robed figures, states that there is no nudity in this look, keeps cherubs but marks them clothed and optional, drops the named painters in favour of the high-baroque manner, and adds `nudity, bare skin, undraped figures, bare shoulders, bare torso` to the negative prompt.

## Acceptance rules specific to this collection

Original subjects only. The figures in these prompts are anonymous and are described from scratch; no real person, no recognisable living individual and no copied composition may appear, and a returned image that reproduces a known painting's arrangement must be rejected rather than committed. The three style previews must carry no word in any language: a returned image with readable lettering anywhere in it is unusable. The lettering swatch is the opposite case — its two lines must be spelled exactly as written, correctly tracked, unclipped at both ends, and with no third line and no ornament of any kind. Divine attributes must be drawn as real objects rather than as floating symbols, and no invented insignia, coat of arms or emblem may be added to any of the four.
